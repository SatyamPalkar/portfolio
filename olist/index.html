Playbook

1 · Owner
Logistics / carrier management lead (owner to be confirmed by the business).
2 · Action
For Priority states: review carrier performance and the estimated-date setting per state; consider longer promised dates where carriers cannot meet them.
3 · Trigger
A state's monthly late rate is 3+ pp above the network rate for 2 consecutive months.
4 · Success measure
Excess late orders and late rate per state, monthly; guardrail: avg review and delivered-order volume.
5 · Next investigation
Seller & Fulfilment shows most extra delay is carrier transit. Next: carrier-level performance on the worst routes (e.g. SP → RJ).

Recommendations are hypotheses from measured data, not proven causes.

Data & method

Scope: 96,478 delivered orders purchased 15 Sep 2016 – 29 Aug 2018 from the public Olist Brazilian e-commerce dataset (BRL). Non-delivered orders are excluded so sales are not counted for unfulfilled orders.

Definitions: merchandise sales = item price excl. freight · late = delivered after the estimated date (orders with both dates; 8 excluded) · network late rate 8.11% · excess late orders = late orders − orders × network rate · tiers (states with 300+ orders): Priority = 3+ pp above network and 50+ excess, Watch = 1+ pp above, Monitor otherwise · dispatch = purchase → carrier hand-off, transit = hand-off → customer (197 orders with missing or out-of-sequence dates excluded; 96,281 used) · seller and route figures use order × seller grain (8.0% network).

Pipeline: CSV → PostgreSQL warehouse → single-grain extracts → aggregated JSON (no customer or order identifiers). Every figure is checked against an independent recomputation (web/check_data.py). Associations are labelled; none is a proven cause.
